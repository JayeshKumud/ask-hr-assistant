NexaCore United States Visa and Employment Policy

Fictional RAG test document. Not legal, HR, tax, or immigration advice.

Purpose

This fictional policy describes NexaCore's internal process for employment-based U.S. immigration. It is for RAG testing and is not immigration or legal advice.

Sponsorship

Visa sponsorship depends on business need, role eligibility, employee eligibility, compensation, immigration history, and company approval. Final decisions are made by the relevant government authority.

H-1B

NexaCore may consider H-1B sponsorship for qualifying specialty occupations where the role and employee satisfy applicable requirements. Some cases may be subject to an annual cap and selection process.

L-1

NexaCore may consider L-1 intracompany transfers where the corporate relationship, employee history, and proposed role satisfy applicable requirements.

Eligibility

HR reviews business need, job, work location, compensation, qualifications, immigration history, and work-authorisation requirements. Immigration counsel determines the appropriate classification.

Visa Process

Manager submits a mobility request; HR confirms business need; Global Mobility and immigration counsel assess the route; employee supplies identity, education, employment and immigration documents; the company prepares the filing; and the employee completes required government or consular procedures.

Compensation

Sponsored employees must meet applicable legal wage requirements and the company's approved compensation framework. Salary depends on job level, skills, experience, location, market data, and legal requirements.

Costs

NexaCore normally pays required employer-sponsored petition and standard company immigration counsel fees. Personal travel, dependant applications, optional personal convenience services, and non-required document costs may be treated differently.

Work Location

Employees may work only from locations approved by HR and immigration counsel. A material location change can affect immigration compliance.

Change of Role or Employer

Existing immigration permission does not automatically permit a new employer, role, or material employment change. HR and immigration counsel must assess changes before they occur.

Compliance

Employees must provide accurate information and report changes affecting immigration status.
