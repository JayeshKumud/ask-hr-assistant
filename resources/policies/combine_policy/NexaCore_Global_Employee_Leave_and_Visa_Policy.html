1. Policy Overview

NexaCore Global Employee Leave & Visa Policy

Fictional RAG Test Document. This document is intentionally created as a realistic enterprise sample. It is not legal advice and must not be used as an actual HR or immigration policy.

This sample policy is designed as a fictional enterprise document for RAG testing. It is not legal advice and does not represent the law of any country. NexaCore Technologies Ltd. (“NexaCore”) provides technology, consulting, and professional services through teams in the United States, United Kingdom, and selected European countries.

The policy applies to regular employees unless a local employment contract, collective agreement, or mandatory law provides a more favourable entitlement. Local law always takes precedence over this sample policy.

Purpose. The policy defines employee leave, salary treatment during leave, approval workflows, documentation expectations, and company support for employment visas.

Core principles: • Employees should be able to take legitimate leave without retaliation. • Managers should plan work around approved leave rather than discourage employees from using it. • HR determines eligibility and records leave in the HR system. • Immigration support is based on business need, role eligibility, employee eligibility, destination-country rules, and company approval. • Visa approval is never guaranteed because final decisions belong to government authorities. • Compensation during leave depends on the type of leave, employee location, contract, and applicable law.

Definitions. “Paid leave” means the employee remains on normal base salary unless the policy specifically says otherwise. “Statutory” means an entitlement established by applicable law. “Company-enhanced” means a benefit NexaCore voluntarily provides above a legal minimum.

Policy owner: People Operations and Global Mobility. Review cycle: At least annually and whenever material legal or immigration changes occur. Version: 1.0 | Effective date: 20 August 2026.

2. Leave Entitlements

Annual Leave. Full-time employees receive 25 working days of company annual leave per leave year, plus applicable public holidays. Part-time entitlement is prorated. Employees should normally request planned annual leave at least 10 calendar days in advance. Managers may decline dates where operational requirements make the requested period impractical, but should offer reasonable alternatives. Unused leave is handled according to local law and the employee’s contract.

Personal Leave. Employees may take up to 5 paid personal days per calendar year for personal appointments, urgent family matters, relocation activities, or other short-term personal needs. Personal leave is normally requested through the HR system and approved by the manager.

Sick Leave. Employees should notify their manager as soon as reasonably possible when illness prevents them from working. NexaCore provides company sick pay according to the employee’s local contract and applicable law. For sample-policy purposes, the company provides up to 10 working days of full base salary sick pay per rolling 12-month period, after which statutory or contractual arrangements apply. Medical evidence may be requested where legally permitted.

Medical Appointment Leave. Employees may use reasonable paid time for essential medical, dental, or health appointments where local law or company arrangements permit. Appointments should be scheduled to minimise business disruption.

Family and Dependant Leave. Employees may request paid or unpaid time for urgent dependant-care situations. The duration and pay treatment depend on local law and the employee’s contract.

Bereavement Leave. Up to 5 paid working days may be granted following the death of an immediate family member. HR may approve additional unpaid or annual leave where appropriate.

Jury/Public Duty. Leave for jury service or qualifying public duties is administered according to local legal requirements. Employees must notify HR promptly when they receive an official notice.

3. Parental, Adoption and Special Leave

Maternity Leave. An eligible employee may take maternity leave under the law applicable to the employing entity. NexaCore will maintain employment rights during statutory maternity leave and provide company-enhanced pay where specified in the employee’s contract. As a sample global benefit, NexaCore provides 16 weeks at 100% base salary, followed by statutory or contractual maternity pay for the remaining eligible period, subject to local law. The employee should notify HR as early as practicable and provide the required medical or birth-related documentation.

Paternity/Partner Leave. An eligible parent or partner may take up to 4 weeks of company paternity/partner leave. The first 2 weeks are paid at 100% base salary and the remaining 2 weeks are paid according to local statutory or contractual arrangements. Leave may be taken consecutively or in approved blocks where local law permits.

Shared Parental Leave. Where available under local law, parents may use shared parental leave. HR will confirm the employee’s statutory entitlement, notice requirements, and salary treatment.

Adoption Leave. An employee who becomes the primary adopter may receive leave equivalent to the applicable maternity/primary-adopter entitlement. A partner may qualify for paternity/partner leave.

Neonatal Care Leave. Where local law provides a specific neonatal-care entitlement, NexaCore will administer it in accordance with the relevant rules. HR will confirm whether company-enhanced pay applies.

Parental Bereavement Leave. Eligible employees may receive leave following the death of a child or qualifying pregnancy loss, subject to local law and company policy.

Unpaid Parental Leave. Employees may request unpaid parental leave to care for a child. Approval and duration are subject to local statutory rules.

Return to Work. Before returning from extended leave, HR and the manager should agree a return-to-work plan, workload expectations, equipment needs, and any legally required adjustments.

4. Salary and Benefits During Leave

Salary treatment is determined by the leave category.

Annual leave: 100% normal base salary.

Personal leave: 100% normal base salary for approved company-paid personal days.

Company sick pay: 100% base salary for the company-paid sick-leave allowance described in this sample policy, subject to local law and contract.

Statutory sick pay: paid according to the applicable jurisdiction. It may be lower than normal salary.

Maternity/primary-adopter leave: company-enhanced pay applies for the company-paid period described in the employee’s contract; statutory or government payments apply thereafter where relevant.

Paternity/partner leave: company-paid entitlement applies for the enhanced period; statutory arrangements apply thereafter.

Unpaid leave: no base salary is paid for the approved unpaid period. Benefits and pension treatment depend on the local plan and law.

Bonus. Annual or performance bonuses are generally subject to the applicable bonus plan. Periods of leave may affect eligibility or pro-rating where permitted by law and the plan rules. HR must confirm the treatment before an employee makes a financial decision based on a bonus expectation.

Equity. Equity awards continue to be governed by the relevant equity plan and grant documents. Vesting during leave may be affected by local law, plan rules, or employment status.

Pension and benefits. Pension contributions, insurance, and other benefits continue or pause according to the employee’s local plan and applicable law.

Salary reviews. Approved statutory or company leave will not, by itself, be treated as misconduct or a performance failure. Salary-review eligibility remains governed by the normal compensation process and local law.

Payroll timing. Employees should continue to receive payroll according to the normal payroll schedule unless a statutory or unpaid arrangement requires a change. HR should communicate any expected change before the leave begins.

5. United States Visa and Employment Policy

This section is a fictional company mobility policy. U.S. immigration rules are complex and change frequently. HR and qualified immigration counsel must confirm the current requirements.

Supported routes. NexaCore may consider H-1B for qualifying specialty occupations, L-1 for qualifying intracompany transfers, and other employment classifications where appropriate. Sponsorship is based on business need and legal eligibility.

H-1B eligibility. A role normally needs to qualify as a specialty occupation and the employee must meet the required educational or experience criteria. The employer generally sponsors the petition process. Some H-1B cases may be subject to an annual cap and selection process.

L-1 eligibility. An intracompany transfer may be considered where the employee and the corporate group meet the applicable relationship, employment-history, and role requirements.

Process:

  1. Hiring manager submits a mobility request.
  2. HR checks business need, job level, location, compensation, and internal eligibility.
  3. Immigration counsel assesses the visa category.
  4. The employee supplies identity, education, employment, and immigration documents.
  5. The company prepares and submits the required petition or application.
  6. Where applicable, the employee completes consular processing or status procedures.
  7. HR confirms work-authorisation validity before the employee starts or changes work.

Compensation. The employee’s U.S. salary must meet the applicable legal requirements and company compensation framework. NexaCore normally targets compensation at or above the required wage for the sponsored role. The final offer is based on job level, skills, location, market data, and legal requirements.

Visa costs. NexaCore normally pays required employer-sponsored petition and standard company immigration counsel fees. Personal travel, dependent applications, premium services requested for personal convenience, and non-required document costs may be treated differently and must be confirmed by HR.

Remote work. Employees may work only from locations approved by HR and immigration counsel. A change of work location can affect immigration compliance.

6. United Kingdom Visa and Employment Policy

The UK section is a fictional company policy and should not be treated as immigration advice. UK immigration requirements can change, and HR must verify current rules.

Primary route. NexaCore may sponsor eligible roles under the Skilled Worker route where the company is authorised to sponsor and the role and employee satisfy the applicable requirements.

Eligibility assessment. HR reviews the role, occupation classification, salary, employee qualifications, English-language requirement where applicable, sponsorship history, right-to-work status, and any other relevant immigration conditions.

Process:

  1. Business requests sponsorship approval.
  2. HR and Global Mobility confirm that the role is sponsorable.
  3. Compensation is checked against the applicable legal and internal salary requirements.
  4. Immigration counsel or the authorised mobility team prepares the sponsorship case.
  5. The company issues the required sponsorship documentation.
  6. The employee submits the visa application and supporting evidence.
  7. Right-to-work checks are completed before employment or the relevant change begins.
  8. HR records visa expiry and compliance dates.

Compensation. Sponsored employees must receive at least the applicable legal salary requirement and the company’s approved salary for the role. NexaCore uses job level, skills, experience, location, and market data to determine compensation.

Leave and sponsorship. Employees on maternity, paternity, sickness, annual, or other approved leave remain subject to the terms of their employment contract and applicable immigration rules. HR should be contacted before an extended unpaid absence because it may have immigration or payroll implications.

Change of employer or role. Employees must not assume that an existing visa automatically permits work for a new employer, job, or location. HR must assess the proposed change before it occurs.

Dependants. Dependant immigration arrangements are the employee’s responsibility unless the company has approved a relocation package that covers them. The company may provide guidance through its immigration provider.

7. Europe Visa and Mobility Policy

Europe is not one immigration jurisdiction. Each country has its own work-permit and residence rules. This sample policy therefore uses the EU Blue Card as a common reference framework while requiring country-specific assessment.

EU Blue Card. NexaCore may support an eligible highly qualified employee who has an employment contract or binding job offer meeting the applicable national requirements. The EU Blue Card framework generally involves professional qualifications, an eligible employment relationship, a salary threshold set by the destination country, a valid travel document, and health-insurance requirements. The exact rules and salary thresholds differ by country.

Common destination examples. Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Spain, Italy, and other participating EU Member States may have national highly skilled-worker routes in addition to or instead of the EU Blue Card. Denmark and Ireland are outside the EU Blue Card framework and require separate national immigration routes.

Process:

  1. Select the destination country.
  2. HR confirms whether the company can employ or sponsor in that country.
  3. Global Mobility identifies the appropriate work-permit route.
  4. Compensation and role eligibility are checked against local requirements.
  5. Employee documentation is collected.
  6. Employer and/or employee submits the required application to the national authority.
  7. Visa or residence formalities are completed where required.
  8. Right-to-work and local registration checks are completed.

Compensation. Salary must satisfy the destination country’s immigration threshold where one exists and must also fit NexaCore’s local salary framework. Relocation allowances, housing support, tax assistance, and travel support are discretionary benefits defined in the mobility package.

Mobility between European countries. Permission to work in one European country does not automatically create a right to work in another. Employees must contact Global Mobility before changing work country.

Travel. Business travel is different from employment authorisation. A person who can enter a country for a short business visit may not necessarily be authorised to perform local employment there. HR should review cross-border travel when work activities are material or recurring.

8. Administration, Exceptions and RAG Reference Notes

Requesting Leave. Employees submit leave through the HR platform. The request should include leave type, start date, end date, and any documentation required by law or company policy. Managers review operational impact and approve or reject within a reasonable period.

Emergency Leave. Where an emergency prevents advance notice, the employee should contact the manager or HR as soon as reasonably possible. HR will classify the absence after reviewing the circumstances.

Documentation. The company collects only information reasonably necessary to administer leave or immigration processes. Sensitive medical or immigration documents should be handled through approved HR channels.

Confidentiality. Employee records are confidential and should be accessed only by authorised personnel. Managers should not request unnecessary medical details.

Policy conflicts. If this sample policy conflicts with mandatory local law, the law prevails. If a collective agreement or employment contract provides a more favourable benefit, the more favourable provision may apply where legally permitted.

Exceptions. Exceptions require written approval from People Operations and, where relevant, Legal or Global Mobility. An exception should document the employee, country, policy clause, business reason, duration, and approval authority.
